Is your backtest real?

TokIO tells you whether a strategy's profit is distinguishable from luck, once you allow for what a Sharpe ratio hides: P&L that isn't independent from bar to bar, and every variant you tried before this one. Every test it runs has a measured false-positive rate.

pip install tokio-ai

import tokio_ai
print(tokio_ai.check_backtest(pf))      # a vectorbt Portfolio, a whole grid included
print(tokio_ai.check_backtest(stats))   # backtesting.py's Backtest(...).run()
print(tokio_ai.check_backtest(tokio_ai.read_tradingview("List of trades.csv")))

The best of 19 SMA crossovers on SPY

Tested alone, the best one is significant: p = 0.038. Tested with the other eighteen it was picked from: p = 0.12, haircut Sharpe 0.21, and a probability of backtest overfitting of 0.90: the in-sample winner did worse than a random pick out of sample in 90% of splits.

What every result reports

  • A test of the mean that allows for serial dependence (HAC, adaptive window), corrected for every variant you tried (Romano-Wolf step-down, the stepwise form of White's Reality Check)
  • The Deflated Sharpe Ratio and Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio, as published and corrected for dependence, and the minimum track record length
  • The probability of backtest overfitting (CSCV) for grids
  • Costs, turnover and the breakeven transaction cost
  • A lookahead check: the Sharpe with every position taken one bar later
  • Concentration (both tails trimmed), consistency across time, and the minimum backtest length

On the TradingView chart

Paste pine/tokio_check.pine under any Pine v6 strategy and the verdict appears as a table on the chart: dependence-corrected p-value, the correction for how many variants you tried, the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, and the minimum track record. Free, no export needed.
